# Internal Thesis Notes: Advanced Nuclear (SMRs & Molten Salt Reactors)
Prepared by: Deal Team - Advanced Energy
Last updated: Q1 2026 (internal working document, not for external circulation)

## Summary of prior thinking

We took a first pass at advanced nuclear eighteen months ago and passed on two SMR
developers at the time, primarily on regulatory-timeline risk - both companies were
assuming a faster NRC licensing path than we believed was realistic given the design
certification backlog at the time.

That assumption has partially changed. One of the two companies we passed on
(referred to internally as "Developer X" pending public disclosure) received a
favorable pre-application engagement outcome with the NRC this past quarter, which
is a meaningfully positive signal relative to our original licensing-risk thesis.
We have not re-engaged with Developer X directly yet, but the deal team flagged this
as worth revisiting.

## Molten salt reactor sub-segment

We have historically treated molten salt reactor (MSR) developers as a distinct
sub-thesis from light-water SMR designs, given the different regulatory pathway and
the added fuel-cycle and materials-corrosion technical risk MSR designs carry. Our
technical advisor's view (informal call notes, not a formal diligence report) was
that MSR designs are technically compelling on paper - better passive safety
characteristics, higher thermal efficiency - but carry meaningfully longer time-to-
commercial-demonstration than single-loop SMR designs, likely 5+ years further out.

## Internal LP sentiment

Two of our largest LPs have specifically asked about our advanced nuclear exposure
in the last two quarterly update calls, citing rising corporate interest in
nuclear-powered data center offtake agreements as a driver of their interest. This
is a demand-side signal worth weighing alongside the supply-side regulatory
developments above - it did not factor into our original pass decision, since
corporate offtake interest in this form has picked up only recently.

## Open question for next review

Should the Developer X NRC signal, combined with the LP demand-side interest, be
enough to justify re-opening diligence on the SMR sub-segment specifically, even
though our MSR timeline concerns remain largely unchanged?
