# Should Northbridge Increase Sourcing Activity in Small Modular Reactors?

## Executive Summary

Light-water SMR developers are now investable at growth stage, driven by regulatory acceleration that directly addresses Northbridge's original pass rationale and new corporate offtake demand that did not exist eighteen months ago. The NRC licensing path has de-risked faster than the firm anticipated, Developer X's favorable pre-application outcome validates revisiting that decision, and LP demand-side interest is real rather than thematic. The thesis is to re-engage Developer X in priority diligence while sourcing additional light-water SMR deals at Series B and later, treating molten salt reactor designs as a separate, longer-horizon bet that remains on monitor status.

## What's Changing

- **NRC regulatory approvals accelerated substantially in 2025-2026** — NuScale's **77-MWe SMR design** received approval in **May 2025**, Oklo's Principal Design Criteria Topical Report was approved **May 2026**, and the NRC rolled out fast-track reforms in **May 2026** under executive mandate.
- **Corporate offtake agreements emerged as a new demand driver** — Google and Kairos Power signed the first corporate agreement to develop a fleet of SMRs in the U.S., and Meta signed a nuclear power agreement with Oklo in **January 2026** for **6.6 GW** of firm power.
- **Global VC investment in nuclear energy startups exceeded $4.5 billion across 81 companies in 2026 year-to-date** as of late **July 2026**, with the U.S. sector seeing **$1.78 billion** in funding.
- **The UK moved from government commitment to contractual execution** — Rolls-Royce SMR signed a contract with Great British Energy – Nuclear on **April 13, 2026** to build the UK's first SMR fleet at Wylfa, backed by **£2.6 billion** allocated in **June 2025**.

## Competitive Landscape: Leaders & New Entrants

**Incumbents / how they're innovating:**

- **Rolls-Royce SMR** — signed a contract in **April 2026** to build the UK's first SMR fleet at Wylfa with **£2.6 billion** in government backing, making it the only SMR developer with government-backed fleet deployment underway in Europe.
- **NuScale Power** — received NRC approval for its **77-megawatt-electric SMR design** in **May 2025**, though it cancelled its first-of-a-kind project in **November 2023** as costs surged **124%** to **$130 per megawatt-hour**.

**New entrants worth watching:**

- **X-energy** (public) — raised **$700 million Series C-1** in **February 2025**, then priced an IPO on **April 23, 2026** raising **$1.02 billion** at a **$10.1 billion** valuation, the highest-valued SMR developer with a public exit.
- **Kairos Power** — signed the first corporate SMR fleet agreement in the U.S. with Google and grew to **533 employees** as of **June 30, 2026**.
- **Oklo** — had its NRC Principal Design Criteria Topical Report approved **May 6, 2026** and signed a nuclear power agreement with Meta in **January 2026**.

## Where Capital Is Flowing

- **X-energy** — Series C, **$700M** (February 2025)
- **X-energy** — Public, **$1.02B** (April 23, 2026)

Capital is concentrated at growth stage and public markets. X-energy raised **$700 million** at Series C-1 then completed an IPO at a **$10.1 billion** valuation, the highest-valued SMR developer with a public exit. No major early-stage rounds have been disclosed for Kairos Power or Oklo, which we read as capital flowing to proven regulatory progress rather than seed-stage technical risk. The U.S. Department of Energy reissued a **$900 million Gen III+ SMR solicitation** in **March 2025**, representing new federal funding alongside private capital.

## Where Sources Disagree

- **Northbridge passed on two SMR developers eighteen months ago due to regulatory-timeline risk**. Since then, the NRC approved two SMR designs and rolled out fast-track reforms under executive mandate — the regulatory environment accelerated substantially faster than the original pass thesis anticipated, and **Developer X** has received a favorable NRC pre-application outcome flagged as worth revisiting.
- **Northbridge's internal view eighteen months ago did not factor in corporate offtake agreements as a demand driver**. External evidence now shows Google-Kairos and Meta-Oklo agreements signed in the last year, and two of Northbridge's largest LPs have asked about advanced nuclear exposure citing this interest.
- **Northbridge's internal deal flow shows advanced nuclear inbound pitches up quarter-over-quarter**. External data confirms global VC investment exceeded **$4.5 billion across 81 companies in 2026 year-to-date**, validating the deal-flow increase reflects a real market shift rather than firm-specific selection bias.

## Investment Recommendation

1. **Re-open diligence on Developer X specifically**, the light-water SMR company Northbridge passed on eighteen months ago that has since received a favorable NRC pre-application outcome. The original pass was driven by regulatory-timeline risk, and the NRC has since approved two SMR designs and rolled out fast-track reforms, directly addressing the concern that justified the pass. The risk is that favorable pre-application signals do not guarantee full design certification or that the NRC backlog re-emerges if political support shifts.

2. **Actively source light-water SMR developers at Series B and later stages**, targeting companies with demonstrated NRC engagement or design certification progress. Corporate offtake agreements from Google and Meta represent a new demand-side tailwind, two of the firm's largest LPs have asked about advanced nuclear exposure, and global VC investment exceeded **$4.5 billion in 2026 year-to-date**. The risk is capital intensity: NuScale's cancelled project saw costs surge **124% to $130 per megawatt-hour**, so SMR developers will require patient capital through commercial demonstration.

3. **Continue monitoring molten salt reactor developers without active sourcing**, maintaining Northbridge's view that MSR designs are **5+ years further from commercial demonstration** than light-water SMRs due to different regulatory pathways and added technical risk. The risk is missing early-stage entry points if an MSR developer achieves unexpected milestones, but current evidence does not justify changing the timeline view.

4. **Track whether corporate offtake agreements translate to dedicated on-site power or remain grid-market transactions**, as this affects SMR revenue model assumptions. A Carnegie Endowment analysis from **June 2026** notes that under Meta's agreement, power continues to be sold to the regional grid rather than dedicated on-site. The risk is that corporate offtake narratives are driving valuations ahead of actual contracted revenue structures.
