# Should Northbridge Increase Sourcing Activity in Small Modular Reactors?

## Executive Summary

The SMR sector is investable now, but only for companies with demonstrable regulatory progress and committed offtake. Northbridge should re-open diligence on Developer X given its favorable NRC pre-application outcome, which directly contradicts the licensing-timeline risk that drove the original pass decision eighteen months ago. The timing is supported by LP demand for firm dispatchable power and record capital deployment across the sector, but the main risk remains regulatory - NRC reform has been ordered but actual licensing timelines are unproven. Companies without near-term regulatory milestones or corporate offtake commitments should be passed.

## What's Changing

- Global venture capital investment in nuclear energy startups exceeded **$4.5 billion** across **81 companies** as of late **July 2026**, putting the year on pace for a record.
- A presidential executive order was issued in **May 2025** ordering reform of the Nuclear Regulatory Commission, and the **2024 ADVANCE Act** directs the NRC to modernize and expedite its licensing process for advanced reactors.
- The U.S. Department of Energy named **10 companies** to fast-track advanced reactor and SMR development, with a goal of achieving criticality by **July 4, 2026**.
- **Google** and **Kairos Power** signed what is reported to be the first corporate agreement to develop a fleet of small modular reactors in the United States, and **Oklo** signed an agreement in **January 2026** for offtake from its future advanced microreactors.
- Multiple LPs have asked Northbridge about exposure to 'firm, dispatchable clean power' spanning both advanced nuclear and enhanced geothermal, framed as a hedge against intermittent renewables' grid integration challenges.
- Inbound deal flow tagged 'advanced nuclear' at Northbridge was up noticeably quarter-over-quarter, attributed largely to the corporate data-center offtake narrative.

## Competitive Landscape: Leaders & New Entrants

**Incumbents / how they're innovating:**

- **NuScale** is listed in New York and raised more than **$700 million** as of **February 2025**, though it cancelled its first-of-a-kind SMR project with UAMPS in **November 2023** after cost overruns and delays.
- **Oklo** is listed in New York, raised more than **$700 million** as of **February 2025**, and signed an agreement in **January 2026** for offtake from its future advanced microreactors.
- **Holtec International's SMR-300** units are now expected to be commissioned by **2030**, roughly a decade of delay.

**New entrants worth watching:**

- **Valar Atomics** (Series B, **2026**) — closed a **$1 billion** funding round in **August 2026** led by **Sequoia Capital**, valuing the company at **$6 billion**, and signed a development deal with **Nvidia** in **June 2026**.
- **X-energy** (Series C, **2025**) — completed an upsized **$700 million** Series C-1 round in **February 2025**.
- **Nano Nuclear** (public, **2025**) — listed in New York and raised more than **$700 million** as of **February 2025**.
- **Kairos Power** — signed the first corporate agreement with **Google** to develop a fleet of small modular reactors in the United States.

## Where Sources Disagree

Northbridge passed on **Developer X eighteen months ago** primarily due to regulatory-timeline risk. **Developer X** has since received a favorable pre-application engagement outcome with the NRC, meaningfully positive relative to the firm's original licensing-risk thesis.

Northbridge's inbound deal flow and LP inquiries suggest rising interest driven by corporate data-center offtake agreements. However, external analysis indicates that under agreements like **Meta's nuclear power deal**, power is sold to the regional market rather than delivered as dedicated on-site supply, and a **July 2026 Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists** analysis argues that nuclear power and SMRs will be unable to meet data center electricity needs within the next several years, characterizing hyperscaler commitments as PR hype. The offtake narrative driving LP interest may rest on a misunderstanding of deal structures and technical feasibility.

## Investment Recommendation

1. **Re-open diligence on Developer X immediately**, given its favorable NRC pre-application outcome directly addresses the regulatory-timeline risk that drove Northbridge's original pass. The combination of this regulatory progress, LP demand for exposure, and record sector capital deployment creates a window to revisit the thesis with materially changed facts. The main risk remains NRC licensing timelines — executive orders and legislative directives have been issued but actual throughput improvements are unproven, and **NuScale's cancellation** after cost overruns demonstrates that first-of-a-kind projects can still fail.

2. **Actively source SMR deals** that combine demonstrable regulatory progress (NRC engagement, design certification milestones) and committed corporate offtake with actual power delivery timelines, not market sales. LP demand for firm dispatchable power is real and the sector is seeing record capital deployment, but external evidence suggests many hyperscaler announcements are structured as market purchases. The main risk is capital intensity — Canada's first SMR project carries a **$15.1 billion** price tag and IEA estimates put EU SMR construction costs at **$10,000 per kW** versus **$6,600** for other nuclear technologies, meaning even well-capitalized companies may face multiple dilutive rounds before revenue.

3. **Monitor the sector for two quarters** to track whether DOE's **July 2026 criticality goal** for fast-tracked companies is met and whether NRC reform translates into actual licensing throughput improvements. If multiple companies achieve criticality or receive design certifications, regulatory risk materially decreases. The risk is that the licensing backlog persists — **Holtec's decade of delay** and **NuScale's cancelled project** both occurred under the same regulatory regime now being reformed, and executive orders do not guarantee execution.

4. **Pass on SMR developers** without near-term regulatory milestones or committed offtake agreements. The favorable external environment does not change the fact that companies projecting faster timelines than their regulatory progress supports are poor bets. The risk of passing is missing a category winner, but the risk of investing without regulatory or offtake de-risking is backing companies that run out of capital before commissioning their first unit.
