# Should Northbridge Increase Sourcing Activity in Green Hydrogen Electrolyzers?

## Executive Summary

Green hydrogen electrolyzers present a monitor-and-wait opportunity, not an immediate investment. Section 45V tax credits created a 2027 construction deadline that triggered both genuine project momentum and forced market selection, but major cancellations by Air Products and BP in 2025 demonstrate that subsidy-dependent economics remain fragile. Real-world electrolyzer costs running 60-300% above published projections and only 10% of announced 2030 capacity having identified buyers indicate the sector is still working through fundamental commercial viability questions. Active monitoring makes sense given large projects reaching FID and substantial venture capital flowing to efficiency-focused new entrants, but capital deployment should wait for clearer evidence that projects can pencil without maximum subsidy support.

## What's Changing

- U.S. Treasury and IRS released final rules for Section 45V Clean Hydrogen Production Tax Credit on **January 3, 2025**, offering up to **$3.00/kg** as a 10-year incentive, but revised law in mid-2025 limited eligibility to facilities beginning construction before **end of 2027**.
- Major project cancellations occurred in 2025-2026: **Air Products** exited three U.S. projects including a **$500M Massena plant** in **February 2025** after regulatory changes made existing hydroelectric power ineligible for tax credits, and **BP** cancelled the **1.5 GW Duqm project** in Oman in **December 2025**.
- Three large-scale green hydrogen projects reached Final Investment Decision in **2026**: **Orica's Hunter Valley Hub (50 MW)**, largest Australian renewable hydrogen project, the **Barrow project (30 MW)**, and movement toward FEED for **Hy2gen's 275 MW Courant project**.
- PEM electrolyzer capital costs fell from roughly **$1,200-$1,500/kW in 2020** to approximately **$700-$1,000/kW by 2026**, though real-world 2024 system costs averaging **$3,000/kW** suggest actual costs remain **60-300% higher** than commonly cited projections.
- First major long-term offtake agreement signed in **March 2025** between **RWE and TotalEnergies** for **30,000 metric tons/year over 15 years**, while only **10% of announced capacity** planned by 2030 has identified buyers despite **255 developers** actively negotiating agreements.

## Competitive Landscape: Leaders & New Entrants

**Incumbents / how they're innovating:**

- **Plug Power** secured three major electrolyzer supply contracts in 2026, including the largest Australian renewable hydrogen project (**50 MW Hunter Valley**), a **30 MW Barrow project**, and a **275 MW FEED contract** for Hy2gen's Courant ammonium nitrate decarbonization project.
- **John Cockerill Hydrogen** entered U.S. market with a Baytown, Texas alkaline electrolyzer gigafactory awarded **$34M in DOE tax credits**, designed for **1 GW/year manufacturing capacity** and **200 new jobs**.

**New entrants worth watching:**

- **Electric Hydrogen** (Series C) — raised **$380M** in an oversubscribed Series C in **October 2023**, bringing total funding to approximately **$600M** from investors including Energy Impact Partners, Fifth Wall, and Fortescue.
- **Hysata** (Series B) — achieved **95% system efficiency** at commercial scale with capillary-fed electrolyser technology versus roughly **75% for existing commercial electrolysers**, confirmed in peer-reviewed research showing **39.4-41.6 kWh/kg H₂** at 0.8 A/cm². Raised **$111M Series B** in **May 2024**.

## Where Capital Is Flowing

- **Electric Hydrogen** — Series C, **$380M** (2023-10-03)
- **Hysata** — Series B, **$111M** (2024-05-09)

- **Electric Hydrogen**: **$380M Series C** in **October 2023** (oversubscribed, total funding ~**$600M**), with strategic backing from Fortescue alongside Energy Impact Partners and Fifth Wall.
- **Hysata**: **$111M Series B** in **May 2024**, coinciding with peer-reviewed Nature Communications efficiency validation.
- Pattern: Substantial seed and Series B/C activity in electrolyzer hardware, with notably large round sizes (**$380M Series C** is unusually large for this segment). No major growth-stage rounds in electrolyzer manufacturers themselves — late-stage capital is absent because equipment suppliers face existential demand risk when their customers cannot secure offtake. Only **10% of announced 2030 capacity** has identified buyers despite **255 developers** negotiating agreements, which explains why strategic investors are cautious about deploying late-stage capital into manufacturers regardless of technology differentiation.

## Investment Recommendation

1. **Monitor Section 45V utilization and project execution** through the **December 2027 construction deadline** to assess whether tax credit economics can translate announced projects into operating assets, with the risk that the deadline creates artificial urgency followed by a funding cliff rather than genuine commercial momentum. The spread between Air Products cancelling projects when regulatory changes eliminated subsidy eligibility and three large projects reaching FID in 2026 suggests the tax credit is necessary but may not be sufficient for project viability.

2. **Follow Hysata's commercialization trajectory** and any efficiency validation by third-party projects, given that **95% system efficiency versus 75% incumbent performance** represents the kind of step-change that could alter unit economics if it scales beyond pilot, with the technical risk that capillary-fed electrolysis faces durability or manufacturing challenges not visible in lab demonstrations. A **$111M Series B** and peer-reviewed Nature Communications validation provide credibility, but efficiency advantages proven at small scale often fail to survive manufacturing reality.

3. **Defer active deal sourcing in electrolyzer manufacturers** until offtake contract penetration rises meaningfully above the current **10% of announced 2030 capacity**, because capital equipment suppliers face existential demand risk when their customers cannot secure buyers. The gap between **255 developers** negotiating offtake agreements and only **10% of capacity** having identified buyers indicates the hydrogen market is still hunting for its demand base, and electrolyzer manufacturers cannot outrun that uncertainty regardless of their technology or balance sheet.

4. **Watch for clarity on real-world installed system costs** through 2027, given that published projections of **$700-$1,000/kW** clash with reported 2024 averages of **$3,000/kW**, because cost assumptions underpin every production economics model and a persistent **60-300% gap** between forecast and reality makes current project pro formas unreliable for investment decisions. DOE targets of **$2/kg by 2026** and **$1/kg by 2031** become unachievable if the denominator is wrong by a factor of two, regardless of subsidy support.
