# Should Northbridge Increase Sourcing Activity in Green Hydrogen Electrolyzers?

## Executive Summary

Green hydrogen electrolyzers face a critical inflection point: regulatory support is in place (45V finalized January 2025), equipment costs have fallen 30-50% since 2020, and industrial offtakers are committing to long-term contracts with equity stakes. However, project cancellations by majors like BP and Air Products in late 2024/early 2025 signal that production economics remain challenging despite policy support. The sector is investable at Series B/C for companies with demonstrated cost advantages and secured offtake, but timing favors a 6-month monitor position while the first wave of 45V-supported projects either validates or disproves commercial viability at scale.

## What's Changing

- The U.S. Treasury Department released final rules for the Section 45V Clean Hydrogen Production Tax Credit on **January 3, 2025**, establishing a **60 cents per kilogram** credit (multiplied by emissions rate) with provisions beginning in **2026** for emissions occurring in calendar year 2026 and after.
- Between **2020 and 2026**, PEM electrolyzer CAPEX fell from roughly **$1,200-$1,500 per kW** down to about **$700-$1,000 per kW**, though some reports indicate rising system costs exceeding **$1,000 per kilowatt** are creating financial hurdles as of early 2026.
- Green hydrogen developers globally have scaled back investments and scrapped high-profile projects due to elevated production costs and weak demand as of **July 2025**, including **BP's** cancellation of the **1.5GW Duqm Green Hydrogen Project** in Oman (designed to produce **150,000 tonnes of hydrogen annually**) confirmed **December 2025**, and **Air Products'** cancellation of its planned **35 tons/day** green hydrogen plant in Massena, New York in **February 2025**.
- Industrial gas companies are entering into long-term hydrogen supply contracts with equity stakes, as evidenced by **Messer** signing a **10-year** hydrogen supply contract with **Lhyfe** announced **July 13, 2026**, taking a **30 percent equity stake** in four of Lhyfe's renewable hydrogen production sites.
- At least **23 hydrogen-focused startups** globally secured financing in **2024**, totaling over **$1.4 billion** in equity funding, while global investment in clean hydrogen technologies was projected to jump **70%** in **2025** to almost **$8 billion** as of June 2025.

## Competitive Landscape: Leaders & New Entrants

**Incumbents / how they're innovating:**

- **John Cockerill** — leader in high-pressure alkaline electrolyzers with a large global manufacturing footprint.
- **thyssenkrupp nucera** — German-based major manufacturer specializing in electrolyzer technology.
- **Nel Hydrogen** — announced partnership with **Ørsted** on **January 19, 2024** to supply a **20 MW PEM electrolyzer** for a green hydrogen production facility.

**New entrants worth watching:**

- **Electric Hydrogen** (funding stage unknown) — raised **$380 million** in a funding round announced **October 3, 2023**, led by Fortescue, Fifth Wall and Energy Impact Partners, with cumulative total of approximately **$545.57 million** raised to date.
- **Ohmium** (Series C) — electrolyzer maker headquartered in California but manufacturing in India, raised **$250 million** in its Series C round reported **February 2024**.

## Where Capital Is Flowing

- **Electric Hydrogen** — Unknown, **$380M** (2023-10-03), led by **Fortescue, Fifth Wall and Energy Impact Partners**
- **Ohmium** — Series C, **$250M** (2024-02)

Capital remains concentrated at growth stage (**$250M-$380M** rounds) with strategic and climate-focused investors active, but the **$1.4 billion** in 2024 startup funding across **23 companies** indicates a crowded field where cost differentiation will be difficult to defend. The presence of strategic investors (industrial gas companies) signals conviction in long-term offtake structures, but the absence of follow-on rounds for earlier-stage companies suggests investors are waiting for commercial validation from the first wave of 45V-supported projects before deploying additional capital.

## Investment Recommendation

1. **Monitor for two quarters** while the first wave of 45V-supported projects (credit provisions beginning **2026**) either validates production economics or reveals persistent cost barriers, with the risk that weak demand extends project timelines beyond our fund cycle. The contradiction between falling equipment CAPEX (down to **$700-$1,000/kW**) and rising system costs creating financial hurdles (exceeding **$1,000/kW** as of early **2026**) requires resolution through actual project data before committing capital.

2. **Source Series B/C deals** in electrolyzer manufacturers with demonstrated cost advantages below **$700/kW** and secured industrial offtake contracts similar to the **Lhyfe-Messer** structure (**10-year** supply contract with **30% equity stake**), targeting companies in the **$250M-$400M** raise range evidenced by **Ohmium's $250M Series C** and **Electric Hydrogen's $380M** round. The risk is capital intensity: these companies require substantial follow-on funding to reach manufacturing scale, and the **$1.4B** in **2024** startup funding indicates a crowded field where differentiation will be difficult to defend.

3. **Pass on early-stage electrolyzer technology plays and gigascale project developers.** **BP's** cancellation of the **1.5GW Duqm project** designed for **150,000 tonnes annually** and **Air Products'** exit from its **35 tons/day Massena plant** demonstrate that even well-capitalized incumbents cannot make the economics work at scale under current demand conditions. The risk of further project cancellations (global scale-back due to elevated costs and weak demand as of **July 2025**) is too high for venture-stage capital deployment in this segment.
