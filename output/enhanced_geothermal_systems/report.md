# Should Northbridge Increase Sourcing Activity in Enhanced Geothermal Systems?

## Executive Summary

Enhanced geothermal is investable now at Series B and later stages. Fervo's public exit at **$7.4 billion** validates the technology and market, drilling costs have fallen by half in live deployments, and utilities are signing multi-year PPAs at utility scale. The main risks are permitting and interconnection delays, which cause schedule overruns averaging **59%** even as drilling costs improve. Northbridge should source Series B+ deals in this window before late-stage pricing reflects the de-risking that has already occurred.

## What's Changing

- **Fervo Energy** achieved a **70% reduction** in geothermal drilling time, with drilling costs across its first four horizontal wells at the Cape project falling from **$9.4 million to $4.8 million** per well.
- In **January 2025**, the Inflation Reduction Act's investment and production tax credits were expanded to cover geothermal power.
- In **November 2025**, the U.S. Department of Energy merged its geothermal office with its fossil energy office, creating a new Hydrocarbons and Geothermal Energy office.
- As of **April 2025**, **20 of the 24 wells** required for the world's largest EGS development (**500 MW capacity**) had been completed.
- Multiple LPs have asked Northbridge about exposure to 'firm, dispatchable clean power' as a category spanning both advanced nuclear and enhanced geothermal, framed as a hedge against intermittent renewables' grid integration challenges.
- Inbound deal flow tagged 'enhanced geothermal' shows the average stage has shifted later (fewer seed-stage intros, more Series B+).

## Competitive Landscape: Leaders & New Entrants

**Incumbents / how they're innovating:**

**Fervo Energy** achieved a **70% reduction** in geothermal drilling time and demonstrated cost reduction from **$9.4 million to $4.8 million** per well across its first four horizontal wells; secured major utility-scale power purchase agreements including **31 MW** with **Shell Energy** and **115 MW** with **Google**; completed IPO on NASDAQ raising **$1.89 billion** at **$7.39 billion** valuation.

**New entrants worth watching:**

- **Eavor** — uses a closed-loop geothermal energy design, distinguishing it from conventional open-loop or enhanced geothermal approaches.
- **Baseload** — secured a **15-year** power purchase agreement with **Google** for geothermal capacity, with deliveries to California's grid beginning in **2026**.
- **Thermalis** (Series B) — uses directional drilling techniques adapted from oil & gas to make geothermal viable outside traditional volcanic/tectonic-boundary regions; Northbridge assessed the drilling technology as reasonably de-risked given its oil & gas heritage.

Capital is concentrating in one proven winner rather than distributing across a maturing cohort. **Fervo** raised **$462 million** in **December 2025** and IPO'd five months later at a **$7.4 billion** valuation, while the broader sector saw only **$106 million** raised across **3 rounds** in **January-February 2026**. No other company has raised a growth-stage round approaching Fervo's scale. This concentration suggests late-stage capital views Fervo as the category leader and has not yet priced in the broader technical de-risking across directional-drilling approaches — which we read as an open window for Series B and C entrants, not a sector that has already run.

## Where Sources Disagree

Northbridge passed on **Thermalis** at Series B, assessing the drilling approach as de-risked. External evidence since that decision shows **Fervo** cut drilling costs **49%** well-to-well and completed a **$7.4 billion** IPO, suggesting the timing of Northbridge's pass may have been at the inflection point where technical de-risking was becoming visible in cost data and commercial traction.

Northbridge's internal tracking noted inbound deal flow shifting from seed to Series B+ as a sign of sector maturation. External funding data shows the opposite: only **$106 million** raised across **3 rounds** in **Jan-Feb 2026**, while **Fervo** raised **$462 million** in **Dec 2025** and IPO'd five months later. This suggests capital is concentrating in one proven winner rather than distributing across a maturing cohort, which changes the sourcing strategy.

## Investment Recommendation

1. **Actively source Series B and Series C EGS developers** using directional drilling adapted from oil and gas, targeting companies with signed utility PPAs or letters of intent. **Fervo's** cost curve and successful IPO demonstrate the technology works at scale, and utilities are signing multi-year contracts. Northbridge already assessed this drilling approach as de-risked during **Thermalis** diligence, and LPs are explicitly asking for exposure to firm dispatchable power. The main risk is permitting and interconnection delays, which cause **59% average schedule overruns** and could push revenue timelines beyond what Series B/C cap tables can survive without bridge financing.

2. **Run full diligence on the two earlier-stage EGS developers** Northbridge tracked informally during the **Thermalis** review, if they have since raised Series B or signed utility PPAs. These companies pursue the same directional-drilling approach, and inbound deal flow has shifted to later stages. The risk is that both companies remain pre-commercial or were acquired by **Fervo** or a utility, in which case this diligence yields no actionable deal but confirms the sector has consolidated faster than the quarterly scan suggested.

3. **Monitor federal permitting reform and DOE geothermal funding deployment through Q4 2026**, specifically tracking whether the **$171.5 million** field test funding flows to companies outside **Fervo** and whether the new Hydrocarbons and Geothermal Energy office accelerates lease auctions identified as a bottleneck. The risk is that the DOE office merger signals deprioritization rather than acceleration, and that field test funding goes entirely to national labs or incumbents, in which case the monitoring reveals a narrowing opportunity rather than a broadening one.

4. **Track whether Gridline Storage's customer conversations** about pairing storage with firm generation translate into a quantifiable procurement pattern among utilities, by asking two other energy portfolio companies to report similar signals in their next quarterly check-ins. If utilities are systematically modeling geothermal alongside storage rather than as competing solutions, that demand-side shift strengthens the case for prioritizing geothermal deals over additional storage investments. The risk is that this is customer speculation rather than budgeted procurement, in which case the two-quarter monitoring window clarifies intent without committing capital prematurely.
