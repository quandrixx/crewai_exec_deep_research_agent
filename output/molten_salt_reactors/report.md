# Should Northbridge Increase Sourcing Activity in Molten Salt Reactors?

## Executive Summary

Molten salt reactor developers are clearing regulatory barriers faster than Northbridge's 2024 technical advisor assessment anticipated, with the NRC's first liquid-fueled construction permit issued September 2024 and Kairos Power securing $303 million in DOE milestone funding by February 2026. Corporate offtake commitments from Google and Riot Platforms have arrived concurrent with this technical progress rather than ahead of it, which distinguishes this from speculative corporate positioning. The sector is investable now at Series B/C stages for companies with federal funding partnerships or corporate offtakes, with regulatory timeline risk as the primary constraint - NRC design certification backlogs remain unresolved and could extend commercialization by 3-5 years beyond developer projections.

## What's Changing

- The U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission issued its first construction permit for a liquid-fueled advanced reactor on **September 16, 2024** (Abilene Christian University's Molten Salt Research Reactor), marking only the second construction permit issued for any advanced reactor design.
- Major technology companies established direct offtake commitments with molten salt reactor developers in 2025-2026: **Google** committed to purchasing power from **Kairos Power** for data center supply (2025), and **Riot Platforms** signed an agreement with **Terrestrial Energy** for a co-located data center reactor (**May 2026**).
- The U.S. Department of Energy committed **$303 million** in milestone-based funding to **Kairos Power's** Hermes advanced reactor project, reported **February 27, 2026**, representing substantial federal capital deployment to a molten salt reactor design.
- Venture investment in nuclear energy startups (including molten salt reactor developers) exceeded **$4.5 billion** across 81 companies in the year-to-date period through **July 28, 2026**, putting 2026 on pace to exceed 2025's record $6.2 billion, with molten salt reactor companies specifically raising **$353 million** in equity across 3 rounds between **January and April 2026**.
- Universities and reactor developers announced plans to site demonstration molten salt reactors at academic campuses: **Terrestrial Energy** and **Texas A&M University System** announced plans in **February 2025** to site an Integral Molten Salt Reactor at the Texas A&M-RELLIS campus.

## Competitive Landscape: Leaders & New Entrants

**Incumbents / how they're innovating:** No traditional nuclear incumbents have pivoted to molten salt reactor development as a core business line. The field consists entirely of new entrants, several of which have reached demonstration scale with federal or corporate capital rather than relying on incumbent partnerships.

**New entrants worth watching:**

- **Kairos Power** — secured **$303 million** in DOE milestone-based funding for the Hermes advanced reactor project and established a corporate offtake agreement with **Google** targeting a **500 MW** fleet by 2035, generating **$10.5 million** in revenue in 2024.
- **Terrestrial Energy** — Integral Molten Salt Reactor (IMSR) design with announced deployment plans at Texas A&M-RELLIS campus and a signed agreement with **Riot Platforms** for a co-located data center reactor.
- **TerraPower** — sodium-cooled fast reactor project (Natrium) in Wyoming that integrates a molten salt-based energy storage system, raising **$650 million** in **June 2025** with participation from Nvidia's NVentures.
- **Thorizon** — Franco-Dutch molten salt reactor developer that raised **€20 million** in **March 2025** led by Dutch National Promotional Institution Invest-NL, bringing total capital raised to **€42.5 million**.
- **Copenhagen Atomics** — thorium molten salt reactor technology awarded **€2.5 million** in grant funding from the EIC Accelerator in **July 2025** for technological development and prototype validation.

No growth-stage ($75M+) equity round has closed in the molten salt reactor segment specifically — the **$650 million** TerraPower raise in June 2025 was for a sodium-cooled fast reactor with molten salt energy storage rather than a molten salt reactor core, and the **$303 million** Kairos Power commitment in February 2026 was structured as DOE milestone-based debt rather than venture equity. The **$353 million** in molten salt reactor equity funding across only 3 rounds in early 2026 signals capital is concentrating in a small number of leaders with federal or corporate validation rather than distributing across the developer field. Strategic capital from the U.S. Department of Energy and corporate offtake partners (Google, Riot Platforms) has arrived concurrent with technical milestones, not ahead of them, which distinguishes this from speculative positioning and suggests the funding reflects genuine regulatory and technical progress.

## Where Sources Disagree

Northbridge's 2024 technical advisor view that MSR designs carry 5+ years longer time-to-commercial-demonstration than SMR designs stands against external evidence showing the NRC issued its first liquid-fueled advanced reactor construction permit in **September 2024**, the DOE committed **$303 million** to Kairos Power by **February 2026**, and multiple corporate offtake agreements materialized in 2025-2026. The regulatory and capital deployment timeline is compressing faster than the firm's informal technical assessment projected.

The firm's quarterly sourcing team attributes increased advanced nuclear deal flow to 'the corporate data-center offtake narrative rather than any change in the underlying MSR/SMR technology landscape,' but external evidence shows corporate commitments (Google to Kairos, Riot to Terrestrial Energy) arrived concurrent with federal capital deployment and regulatory milestones, suggesting the offtakes reflect genuine technical progress rather than pure narrative positioning.

## Investment Recommendation

1. **Actively source Series B/C stage molten salt reactor developers** with either federal funding partnerships (DOE milestone agreements) or corporate offtake commitments in hand, targeting companies at **Kairos Power's** stage — **$10.5 million** in revenue, **$303 million** DOE commitment, Google offtake secured. The regulatory timeline risk remains material: NRC design certification backlogs could extend commercialization 3-5 years beyond company projections, so investment theses must anchor to companies demonstrating both technical milestones and revenue-generating partnerships rather than relying on projected deployment dates alone.

2. **Commission updated technical diligence on materials corrosion and fuel-cycle engineering specifically**, given that the 2024 informal advisor view flagged these as MSR-specific risks extending time-to-demonstration but the NRC has since issued a construction permit for a liquid-fueled design. The gap between the firm's technical risk assessment and the regulator's permit decision needs reconciliation before capital deployment. The risk is that accelerated regulatory progress reflects lower safety standards rather than solved technical problems, which would surface as post-permit construction delays.

3. **Monitor Abilene Christian University's Molten Salt Research Reactor operating license application timeline** and **Kairos Power's Hermes project milestone schedule** over the next two quarters to validate whether the NRC's **September 2024** construction permit translates to predictable operating license issuance or whether the regulatory backlog reasserts itself at the next approval gate. The risk is that construction permits prove easier to obtain than operating licenses, meaning the apparent regulatory acceleration is a false signal and the 5+ year timeline delta the firm's advisor projected reasserts itself at the operating license stage.

4. **Pass on molten salt reactor developers without federal milestone funding or corporate offtakes**, particularly those raising seed/Series A rounds on technology development alone. The Carnegie Endowment's **June 2026** analysis flagging skepticism that hyperscaler commitments may outpace deliverable capacity combined with 2021 technical skepticism on unresolved corrosion problems means companies without demonstrated federal or corporate validation carry compounded technical and market risk. The sector's **$353 million** in MSR-specific equity funding across only 3 rounds in early 2026 suggests capital is concentrating in a small number of leaders rather than distributing across the developer field.
