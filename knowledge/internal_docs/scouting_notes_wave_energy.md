# Scouting Notes: Wave & Marine Energy
Source: Deal team notes from an industry energy summit, this past spring
Status: Informal notes, not a formal diligence memo - internal use only

## General impressions

Wave energy came up in more side conversations at this year's summit than in prior
years, though it remains a small fraction of overall marine/offshore renewable
discussion compared to offshore wind. Several conversations suggested renewed
interest is coming from a narrower use case than "grid-scale wave power": powering
offshore infrastructure directly (oil & gas platforms transitioning to lower-carbon
auxiliary power, offshore aquaculture, ocean sensor networks) rather than
competing head-to-head with offshore wind for utility-scale generation.

## Specific conversations worth noting

- One founder (company not yet named publicly, described as a "point absorber"
  design) mentioned they had shifted their go-to-market from utility offtake to
  behind-the-meter offshore industrial customers specifically because utility
  procurement timelines were too slow relative to their burn rate. This is a
  go-to-market pivot worth watching for elsewhere in the sub-segment.
- A separate conversation with an offshore wind developer's business development
  lead suggested some larger offshore wind players are watching wave energy as a
  potential co-location technology (sharing existing offshore substation and
  transmission infrastructure) rather than as a competing standalone technology -
  this could meaningfully change the economics if it plays out, since shared
  infrastructure would remove one of wave energy's biggest cost disadvantages.

## Our prior view, for context

We have not run formal diligence on any wave energy company to date. Our informal
prior, going into this summit, was that wave energy remained pre-commercial and too
early for our stage focus. The offshore co-location angle above is the first
argument we've heard that meaningfully changes the near-term economics case, since
it wasn't part of our original thinking.

## Suggested follow-up

Worth a scouting call with the point-absorber company mentioned above once they're
willing to go on the record, specifically to understand the behind-the-meter
offshore industrial customer pipeline in more detail.
