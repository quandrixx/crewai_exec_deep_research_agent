# Diligence Memo: Enhanced Geothermal Systems (EGS) - Company Codename "Thermalis"
Prepared by: Deal Team - Advanced Energy
Stage reviewed: Series B
Outcome: Passed (see rationale below) - internal use only

## Company overview (internal notes, not for external distribution)

Thermalis (internal codename pending public announcement) is an enhanced geothermal
systems developer using directional drilling techniques adapted from oil & gas, aiming
to make geothermal viable outside traditional volcanic/tectonic-boundary regions. We
reviewed their Series B raise this past year.

## Why we passed

Two factors drove the pass decision, neither of which was really about the technology
itself:

1. Drilling cost curve assumptions in their model relied heavily on continued cost
   deflation in directional drilling equipment that we felt was optimistic given
   current oilfield services pricing trends.
2. Their offtake pipeline at the time was concentrated in a single state's utility
   procurement program, which we viewed as concentration risk given that program's
   uncertain multi-year funding status.

Notably, we did NOT pass due to technical skepticism about EGS as an approach - our
technical advisor's assessment was that the drilling technology itself is
reasonably de-risked given its oil & gas heritage. This is worth remembering if
we revisit the sub-segment, since the pass was thesis-specific to this one company,
not a signal against enhanced geothermal broadly.

## Broader EGS market notes from the same review cycle

During this diligence process we also informally tracked two other EGS developers
who were earlier-stage at the time (seed/Series A) and did not run full diligence
on either. Both were pursuing a similar directional-drilling approach to Thermalis,
suggesting this may be converging as the dominant technical approach in the
sub-segment rather than one company's differentiated bet.

## Recommendation for next review

If we revisit EGS, weight offtake diversification and drilling-cost sensitivity
more heavily in the model than technical feasibility, since technical feasibility
was not the concern last time.
