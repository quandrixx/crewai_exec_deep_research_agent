# Should Northbridge Increase Sourcing Activity in Green Hydrogen Electrolyzers?

## Executive Summary

Electrolyzer manufacturing costs have fallen faster than most 2022-era forecasts assumed, and three new entrants have shipped commercial-scale units in the past 18 months using a materially cheaper catalyst approach than the incumbents. Capital is still concentrated at seed and Series A, with no major growth-stage round yet in this specific sub-segment — which we read as a timing signal, not a lack of conviction elsewhere in the market. We recommend actively sourcing seed and Series A deals in catalyst-innovation electrolyzer startups now, ahead of an expected growth-stage financing wave in the next 18-24 months.

## What's Changing

- **Catalyst cost, not stack design, is now the primary axis of competition** — three companies have de-risked non-platinum-group-metal catalysts at pilot scale in the past year, a shift from the prior generation's focus on stack efficiency alone.
- Manufacturing cost per kilowatt has dropped an estimated **35-40%** since 2022 across the segment, driven primarily by catalyst substitution rather than manufacturing scale.
- Two governments' industrial policy announcements in the past year introduced production tax credits specifically tied to domestic electrolyzer manufacturing, shifting where new manufacturing capacity is being sited.
- Offtake agreements have started appearing earlier in company lifecycles than the prior generation of hydrogen startups — several seed-stage companies now report signed pilot offtake letters, not just LOIs.

## Competitive Landscape: Leaders & New Entrants

**Incumbents / how they're innovating:** The two largest established electrolyzer manufacturers have both announced catalyst R&D partnerships with materials-science startups in the past year rather than developing non-PGM catalysts fully in-house — a notable shift from their historically vertically-integrated approach, and a signal they view catalyst innovation as moving faster outside their own labs.

**New entrants worth watching:**
- **Company A** (Series A, 2024) — non-PGM catalyst, first commercial pilot deployment reported this year.
- **Company B** (seed, 2023) — modular electrolyzer design targeting distributed/on-site hydrogen production rather than centralized plants, a distinct go-to-market from the incumbents.
- **Company C** (Series A, 2023) — catalyst licensing model, selling IP to existing manufacturers rather than building its own hardware — worth watching as a potential acquisition target for an incumbent rather than a standalone scale play.

## Where Sources Disagree

Internal portfolio notes from an adjacent hydrogen infrastructure investment (18 months ago) assumed catalyst innovation would remain incremental through the mid-2020s. External technical diligence conducted this quarter suggests the cost curve has moved meaningfully faster than that assumption, driven by the non-PGM catalyst breakthroughs above. We recommend updating internal planning assumptions to the more recent external view.

## Investment Recommendation

1. **Actively source seed and Series A deals** in catalyst-innovation electrolyzer startups over the next two quarters, ahead of the expected growth-stage financing wave.
2. **Prioritize diligence on Company A and Company B specifically**, given their differentiated technical and go-to-market approaches relative to incumbents.
3. **Monitor Company C as a potential incumbent acquisition signal** — if a major manufacturer licenses or acquires its IP, treat that as confirmation the catalyst-licensing model is viable and revisit similar deal structures.
4. **Do not wait for a growth-stage comparable to de-risk entry** — the absence of one right now is the opportunity, not a reason for caution, given the cost-curve evidence above.
