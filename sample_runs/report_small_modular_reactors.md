# Should Northbridge Increase Sourcing Activity in Small Modular Reactors?

## Executive Summary

Small modular reactors became investable in the eighteen months since Northbridge last passed. The regulatory timeline risk that drove the original pass decision has materially improved via Executive Order 14300 establishing eighteen-month NRC licensing deadlines, Developer X received the favorable NRC signal the firm was waiting for, and corporate data center offtake agreements from Meta, Amazon, and Switch represent genuine demand at gigawatt scale. The sector is investable now at Series B and later stages where design certification is in flight, with regulatory execution risk as the primary uncertainty.

## What's Changing

- Executive Order 14300 issued **May 23, 2025** mandated **eighteen-month** NRC licensing deadlines for new reactor applications.
- Corporate data center offtake agreements emerged as primary driver, with **Meta** committing to **1.2 GW** in Ohio (**January 2026**), **Amazon** seeking up to **5 GW** from **X-energy**, and **Switch** signing for up to **12 GW** over 20 years.
- Global venture funding for nuclear energy startups exceeded **$4.5 billion** across 81 companies in **2026** year-to-date.
- UK government signed contract with **Rolls-Royce SMR** on **April 13, 2026**, backed by up to **£599 million** from the National Wealth Fund.

## Competitive Landscape: Leaders & New Entrants

**Incumbents / how they're innovating:** **Rolls-Royce SMR** secured the UK government's first SMR contract and agreements with Great British Energy – Nuclear and ČEZ Group for the Czech Republic's first SMR, positioning as lead vendor in European government-backed deployment.

**New entrants worth watching:**
- **Valar Atomics** (Series B, 2026) — raised **$1 billion** at **$6 billion** post-money valuation, partnering with **Nvidia** on a **30-megawatt** nuclear-powered AI facility in Utah.
- **X-energy** (Series D, 2025) — secured **$700 million** Series D to fund manufacturing for more than **5 GW** of projects, with **Amazon** leading **$500 million** investment for design completion and licensing.
- **Oklo** — signed master power agreement with **Switch** for up to **12 GW** over 20 years and partnered with **Meta** for **1.2 GW** campus in Ohio, Phase 1 targeted for **2030**.

## Where Sources Disagree

Northbridge passed **eighteen months ago** on regulatory-timeline risk, citing unrealistic NRC licensing assumptions. Executive Order 14300 established binding **eighteen-month** deadlines, and **Developer X** received favorable NRC pre-application engagement — directly addressing the original pass thesis.

The firm's technical advisor assessed molten salt reactors as **5+ years** further from commercial demonstration than light-water SMRs. However, **Oklo** signed agreements for **13.2 GW** with Phase 1 targeted for **2030**, and DOE's fast-track pilot targets reactors operating by **July 4, 2026**, suggesting some advanced reactor timelines may be compressing faster than anticipated.

Internal sourcing attributes increased deal flow to offtake narrative rather than technology changes. However, **NuScale's** Idaho cancellation in **2023** after costs rose from **$5 billion** to **$9 billion**, and IEEFA's **May 2024** report on cost overruns indicate execution risks remain real despite demand-side narratives.

## Investment Recommendation

1. **Re-engage with Developer X immediately** given favorable NRC pre-application outcome directly addresses the licensing-timeline risk that drove the original pass. The regulatory risk is that **eighteen-month** deadlines prove unenforceable or design certification still delays despite Executive Order 14300, but Developer X's pre-application signal suggests it is ahead on this dimension.

2. **Proactively source light-water SMR developers at Series B or later** with credible corporate offtake agreements and NRC engagement progress similar to Developer X. Target companies partnered with data center operators given **Meta**, **Amazon**, and **Switch** collectively represent over **18 GW** of demand. Capital-intensity risk is significant — **NuScale's** costs doubled from **$5 billion** to **$9 billion** before cancellation — so prioritize developers with manufacturing partnerships or government co-investment.

3. **Monitor molten salt reactor developers for two quarters** pending DOE pilot outcomes and NRC design certification milestones, but do not actively source MSR deals until technical demonstration risk reduces. The firm's technical advisor assessed MSRs as **5+ years** further from commercial demonstration than light-water designs due to fuel-cycle and materials-corrosion challenges, and this assessment has not been overtaken by external evidence.

4. **Pass on early-stage developers (Seed through Series A)** lacking credible NRC pre-application engagement or binding corporate offtake. The regulatory timeline risk has improved but not disappeared — Executive Order 14300 establishes **eighteen-month** deadlines but does not eliminate design certification complexity — and cost-overrun risk remains material as demonstrated by **NuScale's** cancelled project and GLOBSEC analysis showing costs rising to **$14,071 per kW** if timelines extend two years.
