# Should Northbridge Increase Sourcing Activity in Molten Salt Reactors?

## Executive Summary

Molten salt reactors are investable now at the growth stage, with timing driven by regulatory pathway validation and corporate offtake commitments that did not exist when the firm last reviewed advanced nuclear. The NRC's first construction permit for a liquid-fueled reactor in **September 2024** and **Google's** commercial offtake agreement with **Kairos Power** materially change the risk profile from the firm's prior assessment that MSR designs carry **5+ years** longer time-to-demonstration than light-water SMRs. The main risk remains technical execution on materials corrosion and fuel cycle challenges that still lack full-scale validation, but capital availability and customer commitment are no longer blocking questions.

## What's Changing

- The U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission issued its first construction permit for a liquid-fueled advanced reactor (**Abilene Christian University's Molten Salt Research Reactor**) on **September 16, 2024**, representing a breakthrough in the regulatory pathway that had previously been untested for molten salt designs.
- Major technology companies established commercial offtake agreements for molten salt reactor power in **2025-2026**, with **Google** committing to purchase power from **Kairos Power** reactors for data centers and **Riot Platforms** signing an agreement with **Terrestrial Energy** for co-located data center power.
- The U.S. Department of Energy committed **$303 million** in milestone-based funding to **Kairos Power's Hermes** advanced reactor project in **February 2026**, marking a significant federal commitment to molten salt reactor demonstration.
- Molten salt reactor companies raised **$353 million** in equity funding across **3 rounds** between **January and April 2026**, with broader nuclear energy startup investment on pace to exceed 2025's record **$6.2 billion** by reaching over **$4.5 billion** year-to-date as of **July 2026**.
- Multiple molten salt reactor developers announced university campus siting agreements in **2025-2026**, with **Terrestrial Energy** planning deployment at **Texas A&M-RELLIS** campus (announced **February 2025**) alongside ACU's licensed MSRR project.

## Competitive Landscape: Leaders & New Entrants

**Incumbents / how they're innovating:** No established nuclear incumbents are innovating in the molten salt reactor space specifically — this is a new-entrant-dominated segment with distinct technical and regulatory pathways from light-water reactor designs.

**New entrants worth watching:**

- **Kairos Power** (growth stage) — secured **$303 million** in milestone-based DOE funding for the **Hermes** advanced reactor project and established a commercial offtake agreement with **Google** targeting a **500 MW** fleet by **2035**, while generating **$10.5 million** in revenue in **2024**.
- **Terrestrial Energy** (stage unknown) — developing the Integral Molten Salt Reactor (IMSR) with campus deployments planned at **Texas A&M-RELLIS** and co-located data center applications through an agreement with bitcoin mining company **Riot Platforms**.
- **TerraPower** (growth stage) — raised **$650 million** in **June 2025** with **Nvidia's NVentures** participating to advance its Natrium sodium-cooled fast reactor project in Wyoming, which integrates a molten salt-based energy storage system distinct from pure molten salt reactor designs.
- **Thorizon** (Series A) — Franco-Dutch developer raised **€20 million** in **March 2025** led by **Invest-NL**, bringing total capital to **€42.5 million** for thorium-based molten salt reactor development.
- **Copenhagen Atomics** (stage unknown) — awarded **€2.5 million** in grant funding from the **EIC Accelerator** in **July 2025** specifically for thorium molten salt reactor technology development and prototype validation.

Growth-stage capital is active in molten salt reactors now, with the largest round (**$650 million** to **TerraPower**) closing in **June 2025** and strategic participation from corporate investors like **Nvidia's NVentures**. Series A activity remains concentrated in European thorium-focused developers (**Thorizon** at **€20 million**, **Copenhagen Atomics** at **€2.5 million** in grants), while U.S.-based uranium MSR developers have progressed to growth stage with DOE co-investment rather than pure equity rounds. The notable pattern is that the largest capital commitments are tied to demonstration milestones and offtake agreements — **Kairos Power's $303 million** DOE funding and **Google** offtake validate that late-stage investors and government programs view regulatory and commercial risk as sufficiently de-risked to commit at scale. The absence of pure-play venture growth rounds in MSR specifically (versus broader advanced nuclear) suggests capital is either coming from strategic/government sources or being deployed into adjacent reactor designs rather than MSR as a standalone thesis.

## Where Sources Disagree

The firm's technical advisor assessed MSR designs as carrying meaningfully longer time-to-commercial-demonstration than single-loop SMR designs, likely **5+ years** further out. External evidence now shows the NRC issued its first construction permit for a liquid-fueled advanced reactor in **September 2024**, the DOE committed **$303 million** to **Kairos Power's** demonstration project in **February 2026**, and **Google** established a commercial offtake agreement targeting **500 MW** of **Kairos** reactors by **2035**. The regulatory and commercial timeline risk has compressed substantially from the firm's prior view, with demonstration milestones now funded and customers committed rather than purely speculative.

The firm's most recent quarterly scan attributes increased advanced nuclear deal flow to the corporate data-center offtake narrative rather than to any change in the underlying MSR/SMR technology landscape. External evidence shows the NRC issued the first construction permit for a liquid-fueled reactor in **September 2024**, representing a regulatory breakthrough that had been untested for molten salt designs, and the DOE committed **$303 million** to **Kairos Power** in **February 2026**, both of which are technology-pathway changes rather than purely narrative-driven events. The increase in deal flow reflects genuine technical and regulatory progress, not just offtake story momentum.

## Investment Recommendation

1. **Source Series B and later-stage deals** in molten salt reactor developers with regulatory milestones already achieved or DOE demonstration funding secured, focusing on companies with construction permits or equivalent NRC engagement. The NRC construction permit issued to ACU in **September 2024** and the **$303 million** DOE commitment to **Kairos Power** in **February 2026** validate that the regulatory pathway is no longer hypothetical, which was the blocking risk in the firm's prior SMR passes. The main risk is technical execution on materials corrosion and fuel cycle challenges at scale, but companies past the regulatory-pathway-exists question are now investable at growth stage. Target check size **$15-25 million** in companies demonstrating both regulatory progress and commercial offtake visibility.

2. **Monitor Thorizon and Copenhagen Atomics** for regulatory progress in European jurisdictions and prototype validation milestones over the next two quarters, but do not actively source these deals yet. Both companies raised capital in early **2025** for thorium-based MSR designs, which carry additional fuel cycle regulatory complexity beyond the uranium-based designs that secured the first U.S. construction permit. The risk is that thorium fuel cycles face a longer and less certain regulatory path than uranium MSR designs, and neither company has demonstrated the offtake or demonstration-scale funding that de-risks U.S.-based developers. Track whether either secures construction authorization or equivalent European regulatory clearance before re-evaluating.

3. **Prioritize technical diligence** on materials corrosion, fuel cycle chemistry, and passive safety validation across MSR designs if a sourced deal reaches term sheet stage, given that these remain the unresolved technical problems flagged in external skeptical analyses and were the basis for the firm's prior **5+ year** time-to-demonstration view. The risk is that offtake agreements and DOE funding create momentum that obscures whether the core technical problems are solved or merely deferred to demonstration phase. Scope diligence to include third-party materials science review and fuel cycle modeling before committing capital.

4. **Pass on TerraPower's Natrium project** specifically, as the design integrates a molten salt-based energy storage system with a sodium-cooled fast reactor rather than operating as a pure molten salt reactor, which places it outside the MSR sub-thesis the firm has separated for distinct evaluation. **TerraPower** raised **$650 million** in **June 2025** with strong investor participation, but the technical risk profile and regulatory pathway differ from liquid-fueled MSR designs, and the firm should not conflate sodium-cooled fast reactor investment with MSR investment. The risk is misallocating diligence resources to a design that does not map to the regulatory and technical milestones validated for MSR specifically.
