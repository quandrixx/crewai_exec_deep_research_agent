# Should Northbridge Increase Sourcing Activity in Molten Salt Reactors?

## Executive Summary

Molten salt reactors are investable now at the growth stage, despite Northbridge's prior view that MSR designs run 5+ years behind light-water SMRs. The regulatory timeline risk that drove the firm's 18-month-old SMR pass has narrowed materially: Terrestrial Energy received the first US safety design approval for an MSR in May 2026, and Executive Order 14300 mandates an 18-month NRC decision window. Capital is moving - $353M into MSR developers in four months of 2026, utility offtake agreements signed, and LPs asking about nuclear exposure. The thesis is source deals in developers approaching demonstration, with the risk that construction-phase cost overruns and materials-corrosion issues prove out the technical concerns the firm's advisor flagged.

## What's Changing

- **Terrestrial Energy received NRC approval** of its IMSR Principal Design Criteria in **September 2025** and a Safety Evaluation Report around **May 12, 2026**, representing the first US government safety design approval for a molten salt reactor as of July 2026.
- **Executive Order 14300**, signed in **May 2025**, directs the NRC to reach a final decision on an application to construct and operate a new reactor of any type within **18 months**, accelerating the regulatory timeline compared to historical NRC processes.
- Molten salt reactor companies raised **$353M** in equity funding across 3 rounds in the first four months of **2026**, while global nuclear energy startup venture investment totaled over **$4.5 billion** across 81 companies so far in 2026, on pace to exceed 2025's record of **$6.2 billion**.
- The **Tennessee Valley Authority** agreed to buy up to **50 megawatts** of electricity from Kairos Power's advanced molten-salt-cooled reactor, Hermes-2, representing a utility offtake agreement for MSR technology.
- The **State of Texas** allocated **$120 million** to support Natura Resources' MSR-1 project as of **October 2025**, representing state-level public funding for a specific MSR development project.

## Competitive Landscape: Leaders & New Entrants

**Incumbents / how they're innovating:** No established nuclear incumbents are innovating meaningfully in molten salt reactor technology - this is an entrant-driven segment.

**New entrants worth watching:**

- **Terrestrial Energy** (growth stage) - first molten salt reactor developer to receive NRC Safety Evaluation Report approval of Principal Design Criteria in **May 2026** and holds **$289.9 million** in cash and investments as of **March 31, 2026**.
- **Natura Resources** - secured **$120 million** in equity capital and **$120 million** in State of Texas allocation for its MSR-1 project as of **October 2025**.
- **Kairos Power** - secured a utility offtake agreement with TVA to sell up to **50 megawatts** from its Hermes-2 molten-salt-cooled reactor.
- **Thorizon** - Dutch startup advancing the Thorizon One pilot reactor, raised **€20 million** (**$21.7 million**) in **March 2025** led by Invest-NL.
- **Copenhagen Atomics** - received **€2.5 million** (**$2.9 million**) in EU grant funding in **July 2025** specifically for continued technological development and prototype validation of its thorium molten salt reactor.

MSR-specific equity hit **$353M** in the first four months of **2026** alone, concentrated in developers with regulatory milestones or offtake agreements rather than earlier-stage design work. Venture investment across nuclear startups broadly is on pace to exceed **2025's $6.2 billion** record. The funding pattern signals acceleration at growth stage: state allocations (**Texas $120M** to Natura), utility agreements (**TVA** buying **50MW** from Kairos), and growth-stage rounds (Terrestrial holding **$289.9M** cash). The absence of major strategic investor rounds is notable - capital is coming from venture and public sources, not from established utilities or reactor manufacturers hedging their portfolios, which signals either that strategics view MSR timelines as still too long or that venture has moved faster than corporate development cycles.

## Where Sources Disagree

Northbridge's technical advisor estimated MSR designs run **5+ years** further out than single-loop SMR designs in time-to-commercial-demonstration. Terrestrial Energy received NRC Safety Evaluation Report approval in **May 2026**, holds **$289.9M** in cash, and Executive Order 14300 directs the NRC to decide on reactor applications within **18 months** - a regulatory acceleration and capital position that compress the timeline materially from the advisor's estimate.

Northbridge passed on two light-water SMR developers **18 months ago** primarily due to regulatory-timeline risk. Executive Order 14300 now mandates an **18-month** NRC decision window and Terrestrial received the first US government safety design approval for an MSR in **May 2026** - a faster regulatory path than the firm believed realistic when it made the SMR pass decision. The regulatory timeline risk that drove the original pass has narrowed.

## Investment Recommendation

1. **Source deals actively** in MSR developers that have passed Principal Design Criteria approval and hold runway to demonstration, targeting growth-stage rounds in companies positioned like Terrestrial Energy. Executive Order 14300 mandates **18-month** NRC decisions, Terrestrial received the first US safety design approval for an MSR in **May 2026**, utility offtake agreements are signed, and LP interest is live. The risk is construction-phase cost overruns and materials-corrosion issues during scale-up - Terrestrial's own disclosures flag contractor performance and delay risk, and unresolved corrosion problems from the **1960s** Oak Ridge experiment remain live.

2. **Run full technical and commercial diligence on Terrestrial Energy specifically**, given its NRC approval milestone, **$289.9M** cash position as of **March 2026**, and regulatory head start. Northbridge has not run full diligence on an MSR developer. The risk is that materials-corrosion and fuel-cycle technical issues prove real during construction and the cost structure lands closer to the **$3,792/kW** overnight capital estimate than the optimistic **$70M** delivered cost projection, making the economics uncompetitive even with the regulatory tailwind.

3. **Monitor** Kairos Power, Natura Resources, and European MSR developers (Thorizon, Copenhagen Atomics) for **two quarters** to see whether their regulatory and demonstration timelines compress in line with Terrestrial's progress, or whether Terrestrial's NRC approval represents a one-off outlier. The risk is that monitoring for clarity costs the firm entry pricing - MSR equity funding hit **$353M** in four months of **2026** and venture investment across nuclear startups is on pace to exceed **2025's $6.2B** record, so waiting two quarters may mean later-stage entry at higher valuations.
