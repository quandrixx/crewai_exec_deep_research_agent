# Should Northbridge Increase Sourcing Activity in Molten Salt Reactors?

## Executive Summary

Molten salt reactors are investable now at Series A through early Series B, driven by regulatory acceleration and corporate offtake validation that directly address Northbridge's original pass rationale. The firm dismissed this segment eighteen months ago on licensing-timeline risk; since then, three developers have cleared NRC milestones, bipartisan federal reform passed, and Google signed the first utility Gen IV purchase agreement. The materials-corrosion risk remains unresolved in commercial operation, making this a demonstration-stage bet rather than a deployment-stage one, but the regulatory and demand signals have fundamentally changed since the prior pass decision.

## What's Changing

- Three major corporate offtake agreements signed in **2024-2025**: **Google's 500 MW agreement with Kairos Power** (October 2024) and the first US utility Gen IV purchase agreement between **Google, Kairos Power, and TVA for 50 MW** (August 2025), marking a shift from demonstration-only to commercial-scale deployment.
- Multiple MSR developers achieved NRC milestones in **2026**: **Terrestrial Energy** submitted foundational safety analysis (April 2026) and received Safety Evaluation Report approval (May 2026); **Natura Resources** received NRC/DOE safety design approval for **MSR-1** (July 2026).
- DOE shifted to performance-based, fixed-price milestone contracts, providing up to **$303 million to Kairos Power** for its **Hermes demonstration reactor**.
- Bipartisan federal reform through the **ADVANCE Act** (July 2024, Senate **88-2**, House **393-13**) and **Executive Order 14300** (May 2025) accelerated licensing timelines and addressed design-certification backlog.
- European public financing backed MSR developers: **InvestEU** enabled **Thorizon's EUR 20 million Series A** (March 2025); **EIC Accelerator** awarded **Copenhagen Atomics €2.5 million** plus up to **€17 million equity access** (July 2025).

## Competitive Landscape: Leaders & New Entrants

**Incumbents / how they're innovating:** None identified.

**New entrants worth watching:**

- **Kairos Power** (Series C) — first MSR developer with **500 MW Google offtake** (by 2035), first US utility Gen IV purchase agreement, and **$303 million DOE milestone funding** for **Hermes demonstration reactor**.
- **Terrestrial Energy** — advanced **IMSR design** through NRC Safety Evaluation Report approval (May 2026) for Postulated Initiating Events Topical Report.
- **Thorizon** (Series A, March 2025) — Dutch developer, **EUR 20 million** backed by InvestEU guarantee plus **EUR 4 million provincial grant** in consortium with VDL Groep and Demcon.
- **Stellaria** — French startup spun out of CEA and Schneider Electric, raised **€23 million** (July 2025) with backing from Schneider Electric and Exergon, targeting **2035 deployment** of fast neutron MSR.
- **Copenhagen Atomics** — developing thorium MSR, published research (March 2026) claiming salt purity solves historical corrosion; awarded **€2.5 million EIC grant** plus up to **€17 million equity access** (July 2025).
- **Natura Resources** — received NRC/DOE safety design approval for **MSR-1 research reactor** at Abilene Christian University (July 2026) and DOE enriched molten salt allocation (January 2026), developed through **$30.5 million partnership** targeting 2026 deployment.
- **TerraPower** (growth) — closed **$650 million** (June 2025), largest nuclear fission round of 2025, for **Natrium reactor** integrating molten-salt energy storage rather than pure MSR core.

Series A through growth-stage capital is entering, with public sector instruments playing an unusually large role — InvestEU guarantees, EIC awards, and DOE milestone contracts all appearing in the past eighteen months. No major growth-stage round (**$75M+**) has closed yet for a pure MSR developer, despite **Kairos Power's** offtake validation — we read this as an open window rather than a red flag, given corporate offtake agreements preceded the typical growth-stage wave. European public financing is notably active, suggesting institutional validation has moved faster in Europe than in US venture capital.

## Where Sources Disagree

Northbridge passed on advanced nuclear **eighteen months ago** on regulatory-timeline risk, judging developers assumed a faster NRC path than realistic given design-certification backlog. Since then, bipartisan reform (**ADVANCE Act**, **Executive Order 14300**) and multiple NRC milestone approvals (**Terrestrial Energy**, **Natura Resources** in 2026) fundamentally changed the regulatory environment that justified the original pass.

Northbridge's technical advisor estimated MSR designs carry **5+ years longer** time-to-commercial-demonstration than single-loop SMR designs, but **Kairos Power** signed **500 MW offtake** targeting **2035** and secured **$303 million DOE milestone funding** — commercial-scale commitments suggesting the time-to-commercial gap may be narrowing or offtakers are willing to contract despite longer timelines.

## Investment Recommendation

1. **Actively source Series A and early Series B opportunities** in MSR developers, prioritizing companies with NRC pre-application engagement or demonstration reactor partnerships. Regulatory-timeline risk that justified the original pass has been overtaken by bipartisan reform, three NRC milestone approvals in **2026**, and the first utility Gen IV purchase agreement, while **Google** and **TVA** offtake provides demand validation absent eighteen months ago. Materials-corrosion performance in commercial operation remains unproven despite recent claims that salt purity solves the historical problem.

2. **Conduct technical diligence on materials-corrosion claims**, specifically evaluating **Copenhagen Atomics' March 2026 research** positioning salt purity as the solution versus Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists' 2022 analysis that no material has been shown to perform satisfactorily in the MSR environment. This is the single largest technical risk separating MSR from light-water SMR designs, and the firm cannot take a position on investability without resolving whether recent claims represent genuine technical progress or optimistic extrapolation from laboratory conditions.

3. **Monitor Kairos Power's Hermes demonstration reactor milestones** over the next two quarters under its **$303 million DOE performance-based contract**. **Kairos** is the most advanced MSR developer by regulatory progress and offtake validation, making its execution the leading indicator for whether this segment can avoid the cost-overrun pattern that derailed **NuScale's** first-of-a-kind project (November 2023).

4. **Revisit 'Developer X'** (the previously-passed SMR company with favorable NRC pre-application engagement) to determine whether regulatory momentum now justifies investment despite original licensing-risk concerns. Given the improved regulatory environment and lower technical risk than MSR, this represents a lower-risk entry point into advanced nuclear. The firm's edge on this deal may have eroded if other investors also updated licensing-risk priors based on the same NRC signal.
